Humanoid-LAE: Bringing Learning, Examination, and Certification Into Action-Based AI

What if learning programming with AI went beyond asking questions and receiving answers? What if an AI could teach a student JavaScript, examine the code they write, identify their mistakes, test their understanding, and ultimately issue a certificate based on their examination performance?

This is the idea behind Humanoid-LAE (Learn and Earn), part of the Humanoid-LMT model family being developed by openPAT for its AI platform, Lethan AI.

Humanoid-LAE explores how Action Language Model (ALM) technology could support a complete learning and assessment process, from a learner's first lesson to a final examination.

Understanding Humanoid-LAE

Traditional AI assistants can explain programming concepts and generate example code. However, a complete learning experience requires more than explanations. It requires a system that can follow a learner's progress, evaluate their understanding, identify knowledge gaps, and determine whether they can apply what they have learned.

Humanoid-LAE is designed around this broader objective.

Imagine a learner who wants to master JavaScript. Rather than receiving a collection of lessons and being left to study independently, the learner could interact with an AI teacher throughout the learning process.

The AI would teach concepts, ask questions, assign programming exercises, evaluate submitted work, and adjust subsequent lessons according to the learner's performance.

The process would continue until the learner reaches the examination stage.

The focus is not simply on delivering educational information. It is on guiding the learner through a structured process in which knowledge must eventually be demonstrated.

How the AI Teacher Would Work

Consider a student learning JavaScript from the beginning.

The AI starts by teaching variables and data types. Once the student has studied the concept, it asks them to write a small program.

Suppose the student submits code containing an error.

The AI examines the submission, identifies the mistake, and explains why the code does not behave as expected. It can then ask the student to correct the problem or solve a similar exercise.

If the student continues to struggle with the same concept, the AI can revisit the explanation and provide additional practice.

As the student progresses, the lessons become more advanced, covering functions, arrays, objects, asynchronous programming, and more complex programming problems.

The same learning approach could be applied to Python and other subjects.

A central part of the concept is that the learner must participate actively. Writing code, answering questions, correcting mistakes, and solving problems become part of the learning process rather than optional activities outside the classroom.

Examination: Testing What the Learner Actually Knows

Humanoid-LAE is also envisioned as an examination system.

After completing the required learning stages, a student would take assessments designed to evaluate their understanding of the subject.

For programming, examinations could include theoretical questions, code-writing challenges, debugging exercises, and practical problem-solving tasks.

The system would evaluate the student's answers and submitted code against defined assessment criteria.

For example, a JavaScript examination could test whether the student understands functions, can identify logical errors, and can write working solutions to programming problems.

The AI could also ask follow-up questions to investigate whether the student understands the reasoning behind their solutions.

This matters because producing a correct answer does not necessarily mean that someone understands the underlying concept.

From Learning to Certification

The final stage of the Humanoid-LAE concept is certification.

After completing the required examination, a learner could receive an official certificate issued by openPAT, recording their assessed subject, level, and examination percentage.

For example, a learner who completes an advanced JavaScript assessment might receive a certificate recording their final result.

The proposed system could give each certificate a unique identification number and a permanent verification page, allowing others to check its authenticity in the future.

Certificates could cover different subjects and levels, from introductory programming to advanced technical knowledge.

The intention is to create a record of assessed achievement that learners can retain and share.

The credibility of these certificates would depend on the quality of the examinations, the reliability of the grading process, and the standards established by openPAT. Certification would represent performance against those standards, not an automatic guarantee of professional expertise.

The Role of ALM in Humanoid-LAE

Humanoid-LAE is connected to openPAT's work on Action Language Models (ALM).

The central idea behind action-based AI is to move beyond generating responses and towards systems that can carry out tasks within a digital environment.

Applied to education, this approach means coordinating a sequence of activities towards a defined objective: helping someone learn a subject and demonstrate their understanding.

That process can involve presenting a lesson, generating a relevant exercise, examining a submission, identifying an error, selecting the next learning activity, conducting an assessment, and preparing a result.

These activities are connected. Each step contributes to the learner's overall progress.

This is the perspective behind Humanoid-LAE: learning is treated as a process to be carried through, rather than a series of disconnected conversations.

Humanoid-LAE Within Lethan AI

Lethan AI is the AI platform developed by openPAT, bringing together conversational capabilities, research, and action-based digital work.

Humanoid-LAE belongs to the broader Humanoid-LMT model family associated with this development.

Its learning and examination concept represents one potential application of action-based AI: a system that can guide users through structured tasks, evaluate their work, and help them reach a defined outcome.

Programming education is one example. The same general approach could extend to other technical subjects and learning paths, provided that each has appropriate teaching material, assessment methods, and evaluation standards.

As Lethan AI approaches its launch, Humanoid-LAE represents an educational direction for openPAT's broader ALM research.

Conclusion

Humanoid-LAE (Learn and Earn) explores a model of AI-assisted education in which teaching, practice, examination, and certification are connected within a single learning process.

Its objective is to help learners move beyond receiving explanations to applying their knowledge, correcting mistakes, and demonstrating what they have learned.

For openPAT, this is an application of a broader idea: AI should be able to contribute to completing meaningful tasks, not just generating text about them.
